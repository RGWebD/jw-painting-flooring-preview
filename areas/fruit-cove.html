Flooring Contractor in Fruit Cove, FL

Fruit Cove sits about 18 miles from our west Jacksonville base, where Julington Creek meets the St. Johns River. Most of its houses went up in the 1990s and 2000s, so their original floors are now showing their age. Calls here are mostly about cupped hardwood and cracked builder tile. Homes and local risks 1990s and 2000s houses between the river and Julington Creek The census counted 32,143 residents in 2020, up from 29,362 in 2010. Census survey data puts the median home at 2002, and nearly two in three were built between 1990 and 2009. Close to nine in ten are detached family houses.

Those decades shaped the floors. Where the builders put solid hardwood in the main rooms and ceramic in kitchens and baths, both are now failing for local reasons. Damp river air and a slab that never fully dries feed moisture into solid boards from below, so their edges rise above the centers. That is cupping, and sanding it flat while the wood is still wet only creates a new problem. Builder tile has its own weak spot. Where it went down without the perimeter movement joints that TCNA Handbook EJ171 calls for, it cracks along slab cracks or tents up at the walls. The river adds a flood risk too. During Irma in 2017, the National Weather Service said storm-surge flooding downtown beat the 1965 Hurricane Dora record by at least a foot, and Fruit Cove sits on that same river.

Working the SR-13 corridor from Julington Creek south

State Road 13 is the William Bartram Scenic and Historic Highway, which begins at the county line here after crossing Julington Creek. The name comes from orange groves; the Rev. Theophilus Wilson Moore planted an orange plantation along the river in 1873. The Marywood Retreat Center of the Diocese of St. Augustine is also in Fruit Cove. Julington Creek Plantation lies along Race Track Road, east of SR 13. A 2002 business directory described it as a community of about 4,150 acres begun in the mid-1980s, and builders were still selling homes there in the early 2000s.

Typical jobs are diagnosing cupped oak in living rooms, replacing a few cracked kitchen tiles, and swapping water-stained hardwood for engineered boards or plank. In homes this age, repair usually comes before replacement. These are the jobs we are asked for most, and the local reason behind each.

Buckled & Cupped Floor Repair

Cupped or crowned boards are a moisture signal, not a sanding job. We read the wood and the slab, find where the water comes from, and only then repair or relay the affected area.

Popped & Cracked Tile Repair

Twenty-year-old builder tile cracks over slab cracks and tents at the walls. We replace the broken section, bridge the crack with an isolation membrane and add the missing movement joint.

Hardwood & Laminate Flooring

When solid oak is past saving, engineered wood is the usual swap. It moves less with river humidity, and we acclimate it and check slab moisture first, as NWFA guidelines require.

Luxury Vinyl Plank Flooring

Waterproof plank suits kitchens and family rooms where hardwood kept cupping. We test the slab and put down the moisture barrier the plank maker specifies before laying it.

Tile Flooring Installation

Owners replacing a whole run of cracked builder ceramic often move to larger porcelain. We flatten the slab, use an uncoupling membrane where cracks exist and plan proper movement joints.

Water-Damaged Floor Replacement

A leaking dishwasher or a high river can leave water under hardwood for days. We remove the damaged boards, dry and test the slab, and rebuild with materials that tolerate moisture.

Garage Floor Epoxy & Polyaspartic Coating
