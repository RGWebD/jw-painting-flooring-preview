Flooring Contractor in Palm Valley, FL

Palm Valley sits about 21 miles from our west Jacksonville base, strung along the Intracoastal Waterway in northern St. Johns County. Most homes here went up in the 1980s and 1990s. Calls are usually about damp subfloors, floors that can survive a tidal flood, and finishing work before hurricane season. Homes and local risks

Waterfront lots and raised floors along the Intracoastal

The 2020 census counted 21,827 people in Palm Valley. Census survey data puts the median home here at 1991, with about seven in ten built between 1980 and 1999. Only about six in ten are detached houses; the rest are mostly townhomes and condos. That age matters underfoot. A house built around 1991 may still have its first tile or carpet, and whatever lies beneath deserves a look before anything new covers it. Some of the older waterfront houses sit on raised or crawlspace foundations instead of slabs, and damp air under the house works its way into the plywood. We check the crawlspace and the subfloor before any new floor goes in. Soft panels get replaced. Damaged joists are structural work for a state-certified contractor under Florida’s Chapter 489.

Tidal water is the bigger threat. During Hurricane Irma in 2017, homes along Roscoe Boulevard flooded, and the county closed about a mile of the road between Landrum Lane and Canal Boulevard after erosion undermined a small bridge. For ground-floor rooms that could take water again, agency handouts based on FEMA’s Technical Bulletin 2 list tile, vinyl and concrete as flood damage-resistant. Standard wood flooring is not on that list. Older raised homes near the water need the subfloor checked first.

Roscoe Boulevard, CR 210 and the Palm Valley Bridge

Roscoe Boulevard follows the waterway for much of the community, with houses facing or backing onto the water. CR 210 crosses the Intracoastal on the Palm Valley Bridge, a fixed four-lane span that replaced a 1937 two-lane drawbridge in 2002. The county’s Palm Valley West Boat Ramp sits under its west end, off Lori Lane. Neighborhoods include Palm Valley Landing, Plantation Oaks, Odom’s Mill and Dolphin’s Cove. Typical jobs are swapping water-stained carpet for porcelain or waterproof plank, coating garages on low, damp lots, and replacing tile that has cracked or tented on older slabs. Pool decks behind waterfront homes need cleaning and recoating too.

These are the jobs Palm Valley homes call about most, and the local reason behind each one.

Water-Damaged Floor Replacement

After tidal flooding or a crawlspace leak, the wet subfloor has to come out. We pull the floor, look at the plywood and framing underneath, and let everything dry before new material goes down.

Tile Flooring Installation

Porcelain bonded to the slab with thin-set mortar and cementitious grout is one of FEMA’s flood damage-resistant floor assemblies, which suits ground floors near the water where a storm could push it in again.

Luxury Vinyl Plank Flooring

Waterproof plank works over raised wood floors and slabs alike, as long as the subfloor is flat and dry. On concrete we add the moisture barrier the maker calls for, often 6-mil polyethylene.

Buckled & Cupped Floor Repair

Humid air off the waterway makes laminate and wood swell against the walls. We find the cause, whether a missing expansion gap or a damp crawlspace, before relaying the affected rooms.

Garage Floor Epoxy & Polyaspartic Coating

Garages on low waterfront lots can stay damp long after heavy rain. We diamond-grind the slab, test its moisture and pick a coating that bonds to it, usually finished in one to two days.

Hardwood & Laminate Flooring

Solid wood is a poor bet for rooms that could flood. Engineered wood moves less with humidity, and we acclimate it and check subfloor moisture the way NWFA guidelines describe.

Pool Deck, Patio & Lanai Coatings
