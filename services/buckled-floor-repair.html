Buckled and cupped floor repair: what the job covers

A lifted floor is a symptom. Boards rise because something is feeding them water or holding them in place, and a fix that skips the cause only lasts until the next humid spell. So every job has two halves: diagnosis, then repair. On the diagnostic side, we take moisture readings in the boards and in the slab or subfloor. We check the expansion gap at walls and cabinets, and look for leaks at the dishwasher, ice maker line, water heater and exterior doors. On the repair side, we swap out damaged boards, re-lay floating floors with proper gaps, or plan a replacement when too much is lost.

We work on solid and engineered hardwood and laminate and on vinyl plank . If the boards are wet from a flood or burst pipe, the water-damaged floor process comes first.

Cupping

means the board edges sit higher than the middle, because the underside holds more moisture than the top.

Crowning

is the reverse, and it often follows sanding a cupped floor before it dried.

Buckling

is the floor pulling up off the subfloor, often after flooding or a long leak. Vinyl plank does not swell the way wood does, yet floating plank still peaks at the seams when it has no room to move. Flooring makers warn that cabinets set on top of a floating floor pin it down and can cause buckling or joint failure. Missing gaps at walls and tight door casings do the same.

Local weather keeps the pressure on. Morning relative humidity in Jacksonville averages about 89 percent over the year, per 1961 to 1990 climate records compiled by Current Results. Add a slab poured on damp ground, or a house closed up with the AC off during a trip or an outage, and boards take on water quickly. Floating plank pinned by cabinets has nowhere to go but up.

How we trace the moisture before we touch a board

Most failed repairs come from fixing the boards and missing the cause. This is the order we follow on every lifting floor.

Map the damage

We outline the raised area, note which way the boards run and photograph it. Then we ask what changed: a new dishwasher, a storm, a week without power, a new rug or furniture on a floating floor.

Read the boards top and bottom

A moisture meter compares the face of the boards with their underside where we can reach it, and with a dry room elsewhere. A wetter underside points below the floor. Even readings point to room air.

Test what lies beneath

On slabs we use in-situ humidity probes under ASTM F2170. On wood subfloors and crawlspaces we read the sheathing. NWFA guidelines call for checking both the wood and the subfloor, so we do both.

Get the source stopped

A leaking supply line goes to a plumber. High indoor humidity is a job for the AC contractor. Grading, gutters or sprinklers soaking an outside wall get flagged for you. Repairs wait until the source is controlled.

Let it settle, then measure again

Cupped boards often flatten as they dry, so we re-read before deciding anything. Sanding a wood floor before moisture evens out shaves off the raised edges, and the floor crowns once it dries.

Repair or replace, then walk through

We replace damaged boards, free pinned plank and reset gaps, or replace the room when too much is gone. Then we walk the floor with you and leave the readings for your records. A wetter underside points to moisture from below.

What moves the cost of a buckled floor fix

The size of the damaged area comes first. A few lifted boards by a dishwasher is a different job from a whole living room that crowned after sanding. Matching stock matters next. Leftover boxes from the original install make board replacement practical. Without them, a discontinued product can turn a small repair into a room or a whole connected floor. The install method counts too: glue-down boards take longer to remove cleanly than nailed or floating ones.

Floors running under cabinets or islands add work, because the plank has to be cut free or the cabinet toe kick removed. Solid hardwood may need sanding and refinishing to blend repaired boards. A slab that tests wet may need a moisture barrier before anything goes back down. Plumbing or AC repairs are billed by those trades, and the written quote shows only the flooring work.
