Epoxy flooring in Jacksonville: four systems, one method

An epoxy floor is a system, not a single can of paint. A primer soaks into freshly ground concrete, a pigmented body coat builds thickness, and a clear topcoat takes the wear. What goes between those layers decides how the floor looks and how it grips. We install four finishes.

Flake

floors get colored vinyl chips broadcast into the body coat until it is covered, which hides dust, tire marks and small slab flaws.

Metallic

floors swirl pearlescent pigment through the resin for a deep, poured look that suits living spaces and showrooms.

Solid-color

floors give shops and storage rooms a clean, even surface.

Quartz

floors carry colored sand for grip where the floor gets wet. Garages are the most common setting, and our garage floor epoxy page walks through that job in detail. Warehouses, kitchens and auto bays are covered on the commercial epoxy flooring page.

Metallic finishes suit rooms with light foot traffic. What we look for What does Jacksonville weather do to a resin floor?

Jacksonville has a humid subtropical climate. Rain averages around 52 inches a year, most of it between May and September, and the average July high sits near 92°F. A slab that lives in that air holds more water than its dry-looking surface suggests. Moisture moving up through concrete pushes against anything sealed on top. Coating maker Tnemec links blistering to that vapor, which carries alkaline salts up to the bond line. That is why the slab gets tested before we choose a primer, not after a bubble shows up.

Sun and damp air matter as well. Standard epoxies amber under UV, so a floor that catches afternoon light through an open door needs a UV-stable topcoat. Humidity also limits install days: Sika’s data sheets ask for a slab at least 5°F above the dew point, because condensation can cost the coating its bond. Slab temperature and dew point are checked on install day.

From bare concrete to clear topcoat: our install order

Most of the work happens before any color goes down. These are the steps behind every resin floor we put in, whichever finish you choose.

Measure and pick the system

We measure the space, see how it is used and lay out finish samples. A two-car garage, a home gym and a boat shop each point toward a different build.

Test the concrete

Slab moisture is checked with the method the resin maker names, such as in-situ RH probes under ASTM F2170 or the ASTM F1869 calcium chloride test. A high reading changes the primer.

Put the quote in writing

Your written quote names each layer, the finish, the prep and how many days the floor stays closed. You can hold it next to any other bid and compare line by line.

Grind and repair

A diamond grinder with a dust-collecting vacuum removes the weak top skin and opens the pores to the profile on the data sheet, graded on ICRI’s CSP scale. Cracks and pits are filled and ground flush.

Build the layers

Primer, body coat and the chosen broadcast or pigment go down while slab temperature and dew point stay inside the product’s limits. Flake and quartz floors are scraped and vacuumed before the clear coat.

Topcoat and walkthrough

A clear topcoat seals the system, with fine grit added wherever you want more traction. We walk the floor with you and leave the product names and cure times in writing. Grinding opens the concrete so the primer can grip.
