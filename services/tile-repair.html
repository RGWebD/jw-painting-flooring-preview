Tile repair in Jacksonville: what we fix and how far it goes

Tile trouble shows up three ways: tiles that tent up off the floor, tiles that crack, and tiles that lie flat but sound hollow when tapped. Each one says something different about what is going on underneath, so the fix depends on which you have. For a handful of cracked or loose tiles, we cut out the damaged pieces, clean the old thin-set off the slab and set replacements with full mortar contact. For tenting, we lift the raised section, find out why it lost its bond, and add the movement joints it was missing. When most of the room has let go, a new tile floor is the honest answer, and we will say so.

Matching is what people worry about most. Spare tiles saved from the original job are the best source, so check the garage, attic and closets before the visit. Spare tiles from the original job make the closest match. What we look for Why does tile pop, crack or sound hollow?

Tenting

comes from movement with nowhere to go. Tile grows slightly with heat and moisture, and when nothing soft sits at the walls or across a big room, the stress shoves tiles up into a ridge. TileLetter, the tile trade magazine, names sunlight, structural movement and moisture as typical triggers. A sunny stretch of tile beside a sliding glass door is a classic spot.

Cracks

that stay inside one tile usually trace back to a void in the mortar or a dropped pan. A crack running in a straight line across several tiles more often follows a crack in the slab below.

Hollow

tiles have lost contact with the mortar, or never had enough. Under ANSI A108.5, as the Ceramic Tile Foundation explains, interior floors need at least 80 percent mortar contact, rising to 95 percent in showers and outdoors. Voids leave a tile unsupported, which is how hollow tiles end up cracking. Pulling and resetting tile without disturbing the rest

Tile is unforgiving: one slip of a chisel and a good tile becomes a broken one. The work goes in this order.

Tap-test and map

We tap every tile in the room with a hard tool, mark the hollow ones with tape and photograph the layout. Patterns matter: trouble along walls, near doors or in the sun points to missing movement joints.

Track down matching tile

Spare boxes come first. If there are none, we check the back of a removed tile for a maker’s stamp and measure its actual size and thickness. If no match exists, we talk through a border, a transition or a larger replacement.

Cut out the damaged tiles

Grout around each tile is cut out first, so the neighbors are not stressed. The damaged tile is broken from the center outward and lifted in pieces, keeping the chisel off the surrounding edges.

Prepare the slab

Old thin-set is scraped or ground flat. We look for slab cracks and moisture under the removed tiles. Hairline cracks get a crack-isolation membrane before anything new is set.

Set tile with full coverage

Replacement tiles are back-buttered and set in a notched-trowel bed, and we lift one to check contact. Where tenting started, movement joints go in at the perimeter and in the field, following TCNA Handbook EJ171.

Grout, seal joints and walk through

Grout is matched as closely as possible, and movement joints get a flexible sealant rather than grout. We walk the floor with you and explain how long to keep heavy loads off while it cures. Every tile gets tapped; the hollow ones get marked.

Price and paperwork Cost drivers for tile repair, and the standards a fix should meet

What changes the price of a tile repair

The count and spread of damaged tiles set the starting point. Five cracked tiles in one corner take less time than five scattered across a kitchen. Matching comes next. Spare tiles keep the job small. Sourcing a discontinued tile, or adding a border because none can be found, adds time. Large-format porcelain and natural stone such as travertine are harder to cut out cleanly and may need sealing afterward.

Tenting usually costs more than cracks. TileLetter notes that resetting loose tiles alone is often temporary, so a lasting fix may mean cutting movement joints along the walls or relaying the affected section. Pulling baseboards to make room for a perimeter joint, bridging slab cracks with membrane, regrouting a wider area so the color blends, and pulling a toilet in a bathroom each add a line to the written quote.

Licensing, permits and tile standards

Florida does not let local governments require a license for tile and marble job scopes, and tiling is generally exempt from building permits, as decided by the local building official. Confirm with your city or county before work starts. Industry standards define a sound repair. TCNA Handbook EJ171 calls for movement joints at the perimeter and changes of plane. Interior field joints go roughly every 20 to 25 feet, tightening to 8 to 12 feet where tile gets direct sun or moisture. ANSI A108.5 sets the minimum mortar contact under each tile.
