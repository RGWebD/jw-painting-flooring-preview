Rental property flooring in Jacksonville that outlasts the next lease

An owner-occupant picks a floor for looks. A landlord picks it for what it costs per tenancy: how many leases it survives, how fast it can be patched, and whether the unit next door can be fixed from the same box. For most units that points to rigid-core luxury vinyl plank . The wear layer is the number to watch. One major supplier’s guide puts rentals at 12 to 20 mil depending on traffic, and says high-turnover and multi-family units should target 20 mil. The same guide notes that most full commercial warranties start at 20 mil, too.

We settle one product, color and wear layer for your portfolio and note it on every quote. Upstairs condo units get a different check: many Florida associations require approval and a sound-deadening layer before hard floors replace carpet. Carpet usually goes first. Pet accidents, spills and sand worked into the pad leave stains that a cleaning crew cannot lift, and the unit shows tired in listing photos. Laminate is the next weak point in rentals. Its core swells where water sits along a dishwasher, tub or patio door, and swollen edges cannot be sanded back. Thin-wear-layer plank scratches through near entries where beach sand is tracked in. Floating floors laid tight to the walls, without the expansion gap makers require, peak at the seams in the first hot, humid stretch.

What changes a per-unit floor price

Square footage per unit is the base, and identical floor plans help. Ten units with the same layout are measured, cut and installed more efficiently than ten different houses spread across three counties. Wear-layer grade moves the material cost, and a 20 mil plank costs more than a 12 mil one. Prep varies most in older buildings: a slab that needs leveling, old tile under carpet, or a damp reading that calls for a heavier barrier each add a line.

Removal and disposal, stairs, new baseboard and appliance moves are priced separately. Occupied units take longer than empty ones, and second-floor condos may need a sound underlayment. Your quote shows each item per door, so you can compare it with your turnover budget. Matching the plank in your other units also pays later, because a repair becomes a few boards instead of a room.

Florida landlord rules that touch the floor

Section 83.51, Florida Statutes, requires landlords to follow local building, housing and health codes, or to maintain floors and other structural parts where none apply. In Jacksonville, the city’s Chapter 518 maintenance code puts the owner on the hook for correcting violations, even in a rental, and its code-compliance office lists worn or damaged flooring among repairs to make. If a tenant is living there, section 83.53 calls for at least 24 hours’ notice and entry between 7:30 a.m. and 8 p.m. for repairs. Under section 83.49, a deposit claim must be sent within 30 days after the lease ends. Finish flooring generally needs no building permit; confirm with your local building department.
