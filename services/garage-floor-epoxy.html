Garage floor epoxy in Jacksonville: what the job covers

A garage coating covers more than the open floor. The job takes in the parking pads, the strip under the door, the step into the house and, if you want them, the short stem walls along the edges. Each of those gets ground and coated, not painted around. Most garages get a flake system: a tinted base coat, a full broadcast of vinyl chips and a clear topcoat. The real decision is the chemistry. Epoxy is the traditional base, with a long working time and good build over rough concrete. Polyaspartic cures much faster and holds its color in sunlight, though it costs more. Many floors pair an epoxy base with a polyaspartic top.

Garage slabs take abuse a living room never sees. Tires come home hot off I-95 or I-295, park on the coating and cool, and the tread grabs the surface as it contracts. Thin or under-cured films let go first. That is hot-tire pickup, and it ruins plenty of store-bought kits. Oil is the next problem. Drips soak deeper than the stain you can see, and resin will not bond to oily concrete. Sun is the third: standard epoxy ambers under UV, so a door left open on summer afternoons calls for a UV-stable topcoat.

Day one and day two of a garage coating

Most garage installs take one to two days, then the coating needs cure time before you park. Here is how those days go.

Measure, test and quote

Before install day, we measure the garage, look over cracks and stains and test the slab with in-situ RH probes or the method the coating maker names. A written quote follows.

Empty and degrease

The garage should be cleared when the crew arrives. Oil spots are degreased first, so the grinder does not push oil deeper into the pores.

Diamond-grind the slab

A walk-behind diamond grinder tied to a dust collector opens the concrete to the profile on the coating’s data sheet. A hand grinder reaches the edges and corners, and everything is vacuumed.

Repair cracks and joints

Cracks are chased to clean edges, filled with a material suited to how much they move and ground flush. Control joints are treated so the slab can still move without splitting the coating.

Base coat and flake

The base coat goes down while the slab stays above the dew point by the margin the product requires. Flake is broadcast by hand until the wet resin disappears under it.

Scrape, topcoat and walk through

Loose flake is scraped off and vacuumed, then the clear topcoat locks the rest in. We walk the floor with you and leave the walking and parking times on paper. Grinding runs wall to wall, with edges done by hand.

Why two garages of the same size get different quotes

Square footage sets the base. A three-car garage uses more material than a two-car, but the condition of the slab often matters more than its size. Prep is the biggest variable. Heavy oil staining takes extra degreasing. Old paint or a failed DIY kit has to be ground off completely before anything new goes down. Wide cracks, chipped joints and a pitted apron by the door each add repair time, and a high moisture reading calls for a moisture-tolerant primer.

Then comes the system. An epoxy base under a polyaspartic topcoat costs more than epoxy alone, and a full flake broadcast uses more material than a light one. Stem walls, the house step and moving a fridge or shelving each add a line. Our garage epoxy cost guide explains how to compare bids side by side.

Permits, contractor rules and flood zones

A garage coating is finish work. Florida jurisdictions that adopt the residential code’s permit exemptions for painting and similar finish work generally require no building permit for it, though your building official makes the final call, so it is worth a quick check before booking. Florida’s 2021 and 2023 contractor-law changes bar local governments from requiring a license for painting and flooring scopes. A heaved or broken slab that needs structural repair falls under Chapter 489 state-certified categories. For garages in a flood zone, FEMA’s Technical Bulletin 2 counts concrete among flood damage-resistant floor materials, unlike standard wood flooring.
