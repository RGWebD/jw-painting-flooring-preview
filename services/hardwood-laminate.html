Hardwood flooring in Jacksonville: engineered, solid, laminate or refinish

Solid hardwood is one piece of wood from top to bottom. It can be sanded many times, but it swells and shrinks the most with humidity. Engineered hardwood has a real wood top bonded to a cross-layered core, and the NWFA notes it moves less than solid wood. Laminate is a printed image under a tough clear layer, over a wood-fiber core. With laminate, read the water claims closely. The ANSI/NALFA LF-01 swell test soaks samples for 24 hours and measures how much they thicken, which tells you how the core handles water. The AC wear classes on many boxes come from a European standard, not the North American one.

Many older homes here already have the best wood they will ever get. Riverside and Avondale houses from the 1920s often hold heart pine. That wood came from longleaf pine forests that once reached into northern Florida and were mostly cut by the 1920s. When those boards are sound, refinishing beats replacing. Start with what is under the floor. On a concrete slab, engineered wood and laminate are the easy choices. Solid wood can work on a dry slab, but the NWFA wants a vapor retarder and usually a plywood or sleeper subfloor first. On a raised wood subfloor over a crawlspace, solid wood nails down well, provided the subfloor is dry. Then think about how the house lives. A home shut up for a summer trip swings wood through moisture. So does a room facing the St. Johns River, or a kitchen with a leaky ice maker. Engineered plank and good laminate tolerate those swings better than wide solid boards.

Meters, acclimation and fasteners: how a wood floor goes in

Wood is a natural material that keeps reacting to the air around it. Moisture is the usual enemy, so measuring comes first here.

Check the indoor conditions

The AC should be running at its normal settings before the flooring arrives. NWFA guidelines call for wood to acclimate to the conditions the room will have in use, not to a hot, open job site.

Meter the wood and the subfloor

NWFA guidance treats a wood subfloor as dry at 12% moisture or less, averaged over about 20 readings per 1,000 square feet. Solid strip under 3 inches wide should sit within 4% of the subfloor; wider plank within 2%. Slabs get in-situ probes under ASTM F2170.

Build the right base

Over concrete, solid wood gets a vapor retarder and a plywood-on-slab or sleeper system. Engineered wood may glue straight down with a moisture-rated adhesive. Floating laminate gets the barrier and expansion gap its maker requires.

Fasten, glue or float

Solid boards are nailed or stapled into plywood. Engineered planks are glued, nailed or floated, as their instructions say. Laminate clicks together and floats, with trim covering the gap at every wall.

Sand what can be saved

For refinishing, we measure the wood above the tongue first. Hardwood Floors magazine says a floor needs at least 3/32 inch of wear layer to sand, and a normal sanding takes less than 1/32 inch.

Finish and set the care plan

Finish coats go on with drying time between them. You get the product name, the finish used and care notes. Those cover keeping the AC steady and when a light recoat will do instead of a full sand. A normal sanding removes very little wood when the floor is flat.

What changes the cost of a wood or laminate floor

The product is the biggest lever. Species, board width and finish all change the material cost, and for engineered wood so does the thickness of the real wood layer. A thick top layer can be sanded more times, which matters over decades. Laminate floats and installs quickly, which shows in the labor. The base is the second lever. Solid wood over a slab needs a vapor retarder and plywood or sleepers, which adds material, labor and height at doorways. A crawlspace home may need soft subfloor panels replaced first.

For refinishing, the price follows the floor’s condition: how many boards need replacing, whether pet stains go deep, and how many coats you want. Heart pine patches often need salvaged boards, since old-growth longleaf now comes mostly from salvage. Stairs, tear-out of glued-down wood and furniture moving each appear as their own lines.

Permits, licensing and flood rules

Finish flooring is generally exempt from building permits in Florida jurisdictions using the residential code, as the building official decides. Confirm with your city or county. State law bars local governments from requiring a license for flooring work, but joist or framing repairs need a state-certified contractor. In flood zones, FEMA guidance says standard wood finish flooring is not flood damage-resistant, which matters below base flood elevation. Weigh that before choosing wood for a ground-floor room. In older homes, sheet vinyl laid over hardwood may have backing or adhesive that contains asbestos, so the EPA advises testing before removal.
