Luxury vinyl plank flooring for Jacksonville slab homes

Rigid-core plank has a stiff, waterproof center with a printed wood look on top and a clear wear layer over that. It floats, which means it clicks together and rests on the slab without glue or nails. Glue-down vinyl still has a place in some rentals and commercial rooms, and we will tell you when it fits better. The wear layer is the number worth reading on the box. MSI, a large surface supplier, puts 6 to 12 mil in light residential use and 12 to 20 mil in active homes. It reserves 20 mil or more for commercial projects. The coating on top matters too, because it decides how well the floor resists scratches and stains.

Under the plank, the product guide decides what goes on concrete. Some makers want 6-mil polyethylene. Others build the barrier into the attached pad. We follow whichever one your plank calls for, and the written quote says which it is. Wear layer and top coating are compared room by room. What we look for Where does “waterproof” plank fall short in a Florida house?

The plank itself does not soak up water. The floor as a whole is another matter. Water from a dishwasher leak or a wet dog can run through the seams and edges and sit on the slab under the pad, out of sight. Makers say so in their own fine print. One major rigid-core brand’s install guide says its waterproof floor should not be used to seal out moisture. It also says the floor cannot stop mold or problems caused by flooding. Its warranty excludes flood damage and color changes from sunlight.

Sun is the other trap. That same guide warns that long-term direct sun can discolor, fade and buckle the planks, and it tells owners to use window treatments or UV tinting. A west-facing slider in July is exactly that case. Watch for these signs in a plank floor you already have: Seams peaking or lifting in the strip of floor nearest the glass door Gaps opening at plank ends after the AC runs hard for weeks A hollow, bouncy feel where the slab dips under the floor

A musty smell under rugs after a leak that seemed small

Our vinyl plank sequence, from bare slab to shoe molding

Plank is quick to click together, which is why shortcuts tempt people. These six steps are where a floating floor earns its lifespan.

Read the slab

We run a straightedge across each room and mark humps and dips. One common rigid-core guide allows 1/4 inch of variation in 10 feet. Moisture is checked by the method the maker names, often in-situ humidity probes under ASTM F2170.

Match the plank to the room

A sunny great room, a kitchen and a kid’s bedroom may each want a different wear layer. One maker rates only its 22 mil and thicker products for sunrooms, so sun exposure shapes the pick.

Flatten and seal the base

High spots get ground down and low spots get patched or leveled, following ASTM F710 prep for resilient floors. Then the barrier sheet goes down with overlapped, taped seams if the product requires one.

Float the field

Planks are pulled from several cartons at once to mix the shade. Cabinets and islands stay put, and the floor runs around them with its expansion space intact. The room is kept within the maker’s temperature range, with the AC running.

Seal the wet edges

Around tubs, toilet flanges and dishwasher openings, the expansion space is filled with 100% silicone instead of trim. Nothing is screwed or nailed through the plank, because a pinned floating floor cannot move.

Trim and hand over

Quarter round is nailed to the wall, never the floor. T-moldings go in at long runs and doorways. You get the product name, care notes and a box of spares for future repairs. When the product calls for it, the barrier goes down with overlapped, taped seams.

Cost factors for vinyl plank

The plank itself is the first lever. Thicker wear layers, rigid cores and attached pads cost more than thin glue-down products. The square footage matters, but so does how many rooms the floor runs through and how many doorways need a transition. Prep is the second lever. A slab with old adhesive ridges, humps or a dip near the old wall heater needs grinding or patching first. Removing glued tile takes longer than pulling carpet. If old vinyl tile or black adhesive turns up, the EPA advises testing it for asbestos before anyone scrapes it.

Stairs are priced on their own. Some makers void their warranty on stairs, so treads and nosings may need a different product. Moving furniture, pulling appliances and resetting baseboards each add a line. Your written quote lists every one of them.

Permits, licensing and association rules

Florida jurisdictions that adopt the residential code generally treat finish flooring as exempt from building permits, as decided by the building official. Confirm with your city or county, because flood-zone rules on substantial improvements can still apply. Since 2021, Florida law has barred local governments from requiring a license for flooring work. Repairs to joists or framing are structural, though, and need a state-certified contractor under Chapter 489. FEMA guidance lists vinyl sheets and tiles among flood damage-resistant materials. Condo owners should also read their association’s rules on hard floors before ordering.
