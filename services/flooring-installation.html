Flooring installation in Jacksonville, from slab check to final trim

A new floor is more than the boards or tiles you pick. The job covers tearing out the old surface, checking what sits underneath, correcting the slab or subfloor, laying the new material and finishing the edges. Skip one of those steps and the floor tells on you within a season. Our crew installs five kinds of flooring: luxury vinyl plank , porcelain and ceramic tile , engineered hardwood and laminate , and jointless epoxy systems for garages and slab rooms. We suggest a material only after seeing the rooms, the subfloor and how the house gets used. A beach rental with sand and wet feet needs a different answer than a quiet bedroom wing.

A slab-on-grade house sits on a concrete pad poured straight onto the ground. Concrete holds water and releases it as vapor, even when the surface looks dry. Put a glue-down plank or solid wood over a damp slab and the adhesive lets go, the boards cup, or a musty smell shows up under the rugs. Our humidity adds to it. A house closed up for a summer trip, or a sunny run of floor beside a sliding glass door, swings the floor through heat and moisture faster than most product instructions assume. Tracked-in sand finishes the job by grinding down soft wear layers.

How we install a new floor, step by step

The order matters more than the speed. Each step below protects the one after it, and none of them gets skipped to save a day.

Measure and walk the rooms

We measure every room, note doorways, stairs and appliance runs, and check how flat the floor is with a straightedge. This is also when we ask about pets, pool traffic and your move-in date.

Test the slab or subfloor

On concrete we test moisture before any wood, LVP or glue-down product is ordered, using the method the flooring maker names, often in-situ humidity probes under ASTM F2170. On wood subfloors we look for soft or stained sheathing.

Write the quote

You get a written quote listing removal, prep, materials, trim and the days the rooms will be out of use. If the slab test calls for a moisture barrier or leveling, it appears as its own line rather than a surprise.

Tear out and prepare

The old floor comes up and the adhesive is scraped. High spots get ground down and low spots get filled with self-leveling compound. The vapor barrier or membrane goes down only on a clean, flat surface.

Install to the maker’s rules

Material acclimates as its instructions say. Floating floors keep their expansion gap at every wall. Tile gets movement joints at the perimeter and the field spacing the TCNA Handbook calls for.

Finish and walk through

Transitions, thresholds and trim go back on, and we walk the floor with you, looking for lippage, gaps and loose edges. You leave with the product name and care notes for your records. Low spots get self-leveler so the new floor sits flat.

What moves the price on a floor job

Square footage sets the baseline, but layout and prep move the number more than most people expect. A plain rectangle installs faster than a kitchen with an island, three doorways and a pantry. Material is the second lever: vinyl plank, porcelain, engineered wood and epoxy all carry different material and labor costs. Prep is the third. A slab that needs grinding, leveling or a moisture barrier adds work, and so does rotten subfloor found under an old toilet or dishwasher.

Removal and disposal, stairs, new baseboards and moving heavy furniture each add their own line. Whether the house is empty or lived-in matters too, because working around a family takes longer than working in a vacant rental. Your quote spells all of it out, and the price you approve is the price for the scope on that page.

Permits and licensing in Florida

Florida jurisdictions that adopt the residential building code generally exempt finish work such as tiling and carpeting from building permits, as decided by the local building official. Flood-zone rules for substantial improvements can still apply, so confirm with your city or county building department. Under Florida law since 2021, local governments may not require a license for flooring or tile job scopes. Structural repairs to floor joists or framing are different: they fall under state contractor categories set out in Chapter 489, Florida Statutes, and need a contractor holding that state license.
