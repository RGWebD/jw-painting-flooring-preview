Epoxy floor repair in Jacksonville starts with why it failed

A peeling floor is a symptom. Before anything gets patched, we work out why the coating let go, because a recoat over the same problem tends to fail the same way. Most failures trace back to prep, moisture, contamination or a product too thin for car traffic. From there, the repair is one of three jobs. Small chips and a few cracks in a well-bonded floor can be patched and blended. A sound but worn floor can be scuffed and recoated. A coating lifting in many places, or one sitting on an unground slab, has to come off down to bare concrete.

Once the slab is bare, the new floor is built like any new install, and our garage floor epoxy page covers that full system. If you want to compare finishes before recoating, see epoxy flooring .

How we repair or recoat a failed epoxy floor

The order matters: diagnose, decide, then repair. Skipping the first two steps is how a floor ends up recoated twice.

Inspect and map the damage

We walk the floor, tap and scrape at loose edges and mark every area that has let go. A tape test, such as the cross-hatch method in ASTM D3359, shows how well the rest is still bonded.

Test for moisture

Where we see blisters or damp spots, the slab gets in-situ RH probes under ASTM F2170 or the calcium chloride test under ASTM F1869. The reading decides which primer goes back down.

Quote the repair in writing

Your written quote says whether the floor will be patched, recoated or stripped, and why. If removal is needed, it is priced as its own line so you can see it.

Remove what failed

Loose and failed coating comes off with a diamond grinder and dust collector. When most of the floor has failed, all of it comes off. Any well-bonded epoxy that stays is scuffed so new resin can grip.

Patch the slab

Chips, cracks and pits are cut back to sound edges, filled and ground flush. On flake floors, patches get the same base color and a matching flake blend so they sit in with the rest.

Recoat and walk through

New coats go on inside the product’s temperature and dew-point limits, usually finishing with a UV-stable clear topcoat. We walk the floor with you and leave the cure times in writing. A tape test shows how well the remaining coating is bonded.

What drives the cost of an epoxy repair

The biggest factor is how much of the old coating has failed. A few chips in a well-bonded floor are a small job. A floor peeling across both parking spots costs more than a bare slab, because the old coating needs its own grinding pass before normal prep even starts. What sits underneath matters too. Moisture readings over the primer’s limit mean a moisture-tolerant primer. Oil-soaked concrete needs extra cleaning. Deep spalls take more filler and grinding than hairline cracks do. A coating over old vinyl tile or adhesive may need testing before any of it is ground.

Then there is the finish. Matching an existing flake blend, recoating the whole floor so the sheen evens out, or upgrading to a polyaspartic topcoat each change the price. Your written quote spells out which option you are getting and what it includes.

Rules that apply to repair work

Recoating a floor counts as finish work. Florida jurisdictions using the residential code’s R105.2 exemptions generally need no permit for painting and similar finish work, though the building official decides. Florida law since 2021 also bars local license requirements for painting and flooring scopes. A slab that has heaved, sunk or broken through is a structural problem for a state-certified contractor under Chapter 489. Grinding falls under OSHA’s construction silica standard, 29 CFR 1926.1153. If old vinyl tile or black adhesive sits under the coating, the EPA advises testing it for asbestos before any grinding.
